The distinction

Only one of these chooses.

Chatbot

It answers. You ask a question, it responds, and it waits for you again. Nothing happens unless you're there.

"Summarize these messages."

Automation

It repeats. You wrote down the steps once, and now it does exactly those steps, the same way, every time.

"Every Monday, email me the week's numbers."

Today
Agent

It chooses. You gave it a job and what you care about. What it does next depends on what it finds.

"Work out what I need to know, and tell me."

💡

Nobody confuses the first one. The last two get called agents interchangeably. That's where the word gets slippery, and it matters: one does what you already decided, the other decides. You need to know which one you're being handed.

How to tell them apart

Who decides what happens next?

Did you lay out the steps, or does it?
You did · automation

You set the order and it follows it, every time. It can still make a judgment call inside a step you put there — read this, decide if it's urgent, file it accordingly. The thinking is real. The route was yours.

It does · agent

You gave it the goal, not the route. What it looks at next depends on what it just found — and you couldn't have written that order down in advance, because it isn't the same twice.

Judgment alone doesn't make it an agent. Plenty of good automations think hard at one step and still run the same path every time. The question is whether the thinking changes where it goes next.

  • Pick this if any input or output is a file on your computer.

Setting it up

A name, a folder, a schedule.

In the Code tab, click Routines in the sidebar, then New routine → Local.

Give it a name. Paste the job into Instructions. Pick the folder your messages are in. Set permissions to Accept edits: it can write its brief into that folder, and anything beyond that asks you first.

Set the schedule to Daily. Save. Then click Run now so we don't wait until tomorrow morning.

⏰

The schedule is an alarm clock, not a script. It wakes the agent up each morning. Everything after that is the judgment test from earlier: it decides what to read, what matters, and what to leave out. A timer doesn't change who's deciding.

What you just made has a name, sits in a list, and can be edited later. That's the difference between an agent and a prompt. And the folder is its desk: it reads what's there and writes its answer back, and nothing leaves your computer.

The job

Give it a job, not a request.

A prompt asks for output

"Summarize these customer messages."

You'd get all sixteen back, shorter. It did what you said. You still have to read it and decide.

A job hands over the route
You are my Customer Signal Agent. Your job is not to summarize every message. Your job is to work out what I, as the owner, need to know.

Read the new messages. Use your judgment to tell noise from support issues, product signals, revenue risks, and genuine emergencies.

Write FOUNDER_BRIEF.md with what needs me now, why it matters, what you recommend, and anything you need me to decide.

Be selective. If it doesn't deserve a founder's attention, leave it out.

Anything you deemed not important, put in a separate section at the end with a one-line explanation of why.

Permission to leave things out is what makes it an agent. The last line is how I check its work: everything it dropped, and why. This whole thing goes in the Instructions box. Then Run now, and we'll talk while it works.

While it works

Teach it what counts.

The instruction I sent named five kinds of thing. Those five are doing all the work, and none of them is technical. They're just the categories you already sort by in your head.

  • Noise. Real, but nothing follows from it.
  • A support issue. One person, one problem.
  • A product signal. Several people, one pattern.
  • A revenue risk. Money is leaving, or about to.
  • An emergency. Something is wrong right now.

Yours will be different. A clinic sorts by "is this clinical." A shop sorts by "is this a refund."

🎯

The line to hold

Anything that reaches a customer, moves money, or can't be undone waits for you. Not because the software is untrustworthy, but because the consequence is yours.

Earn it slowly

Let it act only where you've watched it be right for a while, and where being wrong is cheap. Trust is granted in pieces.

⚠️

The mistake to avoid: wiring an agent into something that sends, deletes, or charges on day one. Start where the worst case is that you disagree with it.

Correcting it

Fix the criteria, not the answer.

There will be something in there you'd have called differently. That's not a failure, it's the first day of a new hire. What you do next is the whole difference between a tool and an employee.

Fixing the answer
  • "Move that one up."
  • You get a better brief today.
  • Next week it does the same thing again.
  • You correct it forever.
Fixing the criteria
  • "Anything about a failed payment is an emergency."
  • You get a better brief today.
  • And every week after that.
  • It gets more yours over time.

This is why the instruction is a document rather than a message. You're not correcting an answer, you're editing the standing job.
