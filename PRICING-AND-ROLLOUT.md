# Pricing & Rollout

Reference doc for what the class series costs, why, and how it goes to market.
Every price below is either (a) sourced from a real, currently-offered competitor
with a link, or (b) explicitly labeled as a judgment call. Don't let those blur.

Researched July 2026. Prices drift — re-check the comparables before a re-launch.

---

## The positioning (this is the whole edge)

Every competing course teaches **"build a product"** or **"build an agent."**

This series teaches **"replace the software you're paying for, and automate the
work you're doing by hand."**

That's a different buyer with a much easier ROI story. A product course sells a
hope. This sells a subtraction the buyer can compute:

- The dashboard (Class 2) replaces a BI seat.
- SEO + AEO (Class 3) replaces an agency retainer.
- Automations (Class 4) replace a Zapier plan and some VA hours.

The market research confirmed this gap is real and unserved. Lead with it. It is
also the justification for pricing *above* the $799 Vibe Coding Bootcamp rather
than at it.

**The sentence for the top of the sales page:** stop paying for software you
could own, and stop doing by hand what could run itself.

| [Claude Code for PMs](https://maven.com/) (Khan & Xiao) | $2,500 | ~2 weeks |
| [Agentic Analytics in Claude Code](https://maven.com/dataneighbor/build-ai-analysts-in-claude-code) | $2,500 | 20 live hrs, 10 sessions, 3 instructors |

[Maven's own pricing guidance](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/6732396-pricing-your-course):
12–20 live hours + capstone → **$1,800–$2,450**. Platform prices range $800–$2,500.
Maven also reports courses priced **$950+ earned 50–100% more revenue per landing-page
visit** than cheaper ones — pricing low does not buy conversion.

**Critical gap in all of them:** none include 1:1. Designlab ($999) is breakouts +
office hours only. Agentic Analytics ($2,500) caps support at group office hours.
Vibe Coding Bootcamp ($799) is community Slack, no office hours at all. Private
delivery is a genuinely superior tier, not a like-for-like substitute.

**Also note:** bundled async support is *table stakes*, not a premium feature. Even
the $295 Maven workshop includes WhatsApp/email support. Don't market it as the
differentiator — the differentiator is that every session is private and paced to them.

## Prices

### Group: $1,300/seat — five classes

Sits above the $799–$999 cluster (correct: five classes + prereq support is more
than any of them deliver) and below the $1,500–$2,500 tier (correct: not competing
against three-instructor cohorts and Maven's brand yet).

### Private 1:1: $3,500

**The ladder — quote $3,500. Do not pre-announce the discount.**

| Tier | When |
|---|---|
| **$3,500** | The price. This is what you say first. |
| **$3,000** | Friends/referral rate. Appears *only* if she hesitates. |
| **$2,500** | Floor. Only if she's paying personally, not expensing. Never volunteer it. |

If you lead with "it's $3,500 but for you $3,000," you've told her the price is
fiction and she'll correctly infer $2,500 exists too. You've negotiated against
yourself before she's spoken.

When the $3,000 concession appears, **trade it for something you want**:
- A testimonial + case study of what she ships (you have zero social proof — worth
  more than $500 right now).
- Two referrals from her network.
- Or she adds the GTM class at the bundled rate.

**Why 2.7x is the right multiple:** [Paperbell's coaching data](https://paperbell.com/blog/how-to-price-your-coaching-services/)
shows group programs are priced at a 40–70% discount off the 1:1 rate — i.e. private
commands **1.7x–3.3x** group. $3,500 / $1,300 = 2.7x, dead center. (For reference,
1:1 hourly benchmarks: Codementor experienced mentors $60–$120/hr; Toptal specialists
$200+/hr; Paperbell coaching $100–$350/hr.)

**Above $5,000 is a different market.** [Kriv AI sells a 4-hour Claude Code discovery
workshop for $5,000](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-fkvrksmhykvmq) and
team enablement at $15–40k — but that's sold to *companies*, not to a student. Don't
price an individual there.

### Bound the support — in writing

> Async questions anytime, plus one 30-minute screenshare per week between classes.

Not because she'd abuse it. Because a close friend's referral is exactly the
relationship where an unbounded commitment gets awkward and you feel unable to say no.

## Maven: yes, to launch

**Maven takes 10%, not 30–50%.** Instructors keep 90% minus Stripe fees
([source](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/5593804-getting-paid)). On a $1,000 seat:
Maven $100, Stripe ~$26, you ~$874.

Self-hosting isn't free either — it's ~3% Stripe plus your time and tooling. So the
real question is whether Maven's cut is worth **~7 points of margin (~$91 on a $1,300
seat).**

For that $91 ([source](https://maven.com/teach)):
- **Distribution** — marketplace exposure to 500K+ learners; they run paid LinkedIn
  ads on your behalf and only take revenue share when *they* drive the enrollment
  (no upfront ad spend).
- **Affiliate partners** for anything priced above $250. $1,300 qualifies.
- **Plumbing** — Stripe + B2B invoicing (matters: corporate buyers expensing a course
  need a real invoice), Zoom/calendar integration, landing page, student analytics.
- **Proof of demand** — every comparable above is on Maven. That's where this exact
  buyer already shops.

**The real argument:** the problem isn't payment processing. The problem is that
nobody knows this class exists and there's no social proof for a series never sold.
Maven is a discovery surface where people are actively searching for this.

### Two cautions before committing

1. **Maven can discount your course up to 30%** via the Student Growth Program
   (twice-yearly site-wide sales, referral incentives). A $1,300 listing could sell
   at ~$910. Either price with that headroom or confirm you can opt out.
2. **Unverified: whether Maven gates acceptance.** Their FAQ raises "Do I need a
   large audience?" and never answers it. Check before sequencing around it.

### The private student never touches Maven

She came via personal referral, needs no discovery, and 10% of $3,500 is $350 handed
over for nothing. Invoice her directly.

## Rollout

**Phase 1 — Private student (now).** Five classes, $3,500. First real delivery of
Classes 4 and 5 (which don't exist yet — she's shaping them). Don't discount for the
untested material; *tell her*, and let it justify why you're taking her on at all.
Payment: half up front, half after Class 3. Not per-session — per-session invites her
to leave after Class 1 with her website built, which is the class with the highest
prereq-support cost to you.

**Phase 2 — Cohort #1 on Maven.** $1,300/seat. The 10% is cheap rent on distribution
and credibility you don't have yet.

**Phase 3 — Migrate off, if you want to.**

### The trap in Phase 3

"Move off later" is easy to say and hard to do. If all your proof lives on Maven's
page and all your students came from their marketplace, you can't leave — Maven owns
the audience relationship by default.

**Migration only works if you build the off-platform assets while you're on-platform.**
From cohort #1, not after:

- **Get an email list off it.** Maven's free "Lightning Lessons" exist to build one.
  Use them. That list is yours and travels.
- **Harvest reviews relentlessly.** The $295 Maven workshop has 41 reviews at 5.0 —
  that's what makes it sellable. Ask every student.
- **Get testimonials that name the outcome, not the class.** "I replaced a $400/mo BI
  tool and my SEO retainer" is a sales asset on any platform. "Great class" is not.
