You've built most of it

Class 4 built look: the brief. Class 5 built decide and act, with you starting it. Today closes the loop, so it can start the next round itself.

⏰ A schedule starts it — nothing more

"Every morning at 7:30" says when to look. It doesn't say what to do. What it does depends on what it finds.

What makes it safe to let it run

Give it authority, not a script.

You can't list every case it will meet. What you can do is say, for each kind of thing, how far it may go on its own:

✅
Handle it

Do it and tell me after. Filing a task, putting prep notes in the handoff.

✍️
Prepare it

Get it ready, and I finish it. A reply drafted in Gmail that I read and send.

🙋
Bring it to me

Only I can decide. Money, contracts, a client who's upset.

🚫
Leave it alone

Don't touch it. Newsletters, receipts, anything from my bank.

🎯

The goal isn't "never involve me." It's "only involve me where my judgment is actually needed." Everything else, it should handle, prepare, or leave.

Added to the directions you wrote last class

Step 1 — write down how far it may go.

Open your Morning Briefing project's instructions and add this section below your directions. Fill in the brackets with your own examples:

When the handoff task runs (on your own, while I'm away):

Handle yourself: [e.g. file a task for anything with a deadline; put prep notes for today's meetings in the handoff].

Prepare for me: [e.g. draft replies to scheduling requests and simple questions; leave them in Gmail for me to send].

Bring to me: [e.g. anything about money or contracts, or a client who sounds upset].

Leave alone: [e.g. newsletters, receipts, anything from my bank].

If you're not sure, don't act — bring it to me in the handoff.
✏️

Start with what you already trust. Use your list from last week — what you asked it to do most. The jobs on it that it gets right go under "Handle" or "Prepare." Anything you still correct stays under "Bring to me" for now.

When it runs on its own, nobody is there to answer

Step 2 — check its limits for when you're away.

Open + → Connectors → Manage connectors again. Last class you could answer "ask you first" in the moment. At 7:30 in the morning, you won't be there.

⚙️ Allow or never

For every action the handoff task uses — reading, creating Gmail drafts, filing tasks — pick allow. For actions you never want, like deleting, pick never.

✉️ Send stays never

Send email is still set to never. That's what makes "prepare" safe: it can leave a draft, but it can't send it. The send button stays yours.

"Leave alone" is about kinds of messages, so it lives in your instructions, not here — these settings are per action. And "ask you first" still works when you're directing it yourself; it just isn't a setting to rely on while you're not there.

So it doesn't rediscover the world every morning

Step 3 — give it a log of what it did.

Each run starts fresh. Without a record, it would draft the same reply twice or forget what's still waiting on you. So it keeps a running log in the project. Add one more line to your instructions:

Keep a running log in this project called "Handoff log": what you handled, what you prepared, what's waiting on me, and what changed since your last run. Read it at the start of every handoff run, and update it before the run finishes.
📒 What the log is for

It's how it knows "I already drafted that" and "that's still waiting on you since Tuesday." It's also how it notices what changed.

👀 It's yours to read

Open it any time to see exactly what it did and when. If something in it is wrong, tell it — it fixes the log and learns from it.

A second scheduled task, right after the brief

Step 4 — schedule the handoff.

Keep your morning brief exactly as it is — it's still the automation. Add a second scheduled task that runs after it. In the project's message box, with Cowork selected, paste:

Set up a scheduled task in this project that runs every weekday at 7:30am, after my morning brief.

Each run: read today's brief and your Handoff log. For each item, follow my instructions on what to handle, prepare, bring to me, or leave alone. After anything you do, check it really happened — the draft is in Gmail, the task is on my list — and if it didn't, say so.

Then write my morning handoff: what you found, what you handled, what you prepared for me, what changed since yesterday, and what needs my decision. If nothing needs me, say so. Save each day's handoff in this project, dated, so I have a history. Update your Handoff log last.

Leave Require this computer off, like your brief. The whole loop runs in the cloud.

Don't wait until tomorrow

Step 5 — run it once, and check its work.

Ask Claude to run the handoff task once now. Then check it the way you'd check a new assistant's first morning:

  • 1. Read the handoff → does "needs my decision" contain only things that really need you?
  • 2. Open Gmail's drafts and your task list → is everything it says it did actually there?
  • 3. Open the Handoff log → does it match what happened?
✅

A handoff, drafts waiting, tasks filed, and a log that matches — tomorrow it does this before you wake up.

🔍

Don't take its word for it. An agent can say "I created the draft" even when the draft didn't save — a connector hiccup, the wrong thread. So after it acts, the task looks for each draft and task it made, and tells you in the handoff if one is missing. Check too, until you trust it.

The first week is calibration

Tune what it brings to you.

Your first few handoffs will be off in one direction or the other. Fix it in your instructions, by moving kinds of things between the four lists — not by editing the handoff.

📥 It brings you too much

You keep seeing things you'd have handled the same way every time. Move that kind of thing to Handle or Prepare.

🤐 It decides too much

It handled something you'd have wanted to see. Move that kind of thing to Bring to me, and tell it why.

Optional, once the morning loop has earned your trust

It can keep watch during the day, too.

"Proactive" just means the loop runs again. Add a check every hour or two: it looks at what's changed since its last run, acts if something did, and updates your handoff.

🔁 What that looks like

"The 2pm moved to 3pm. I updated the prep in your handoff and flagged the conflict it creates at 3:30." You didn't have to notice.

⏱️ It checks — it doesn't get notified

It can't be told the instant something changes. It finds out the next time it looks. Checking hourly means your handoff catches up within the hour.

📊

Checks during the day use your plan. Watch Settings → Usage for a few days. If you hit limits on Pro, check every few hours instead of hourly, or move to Max, where you're unlikely to hit them.

Your new morning habit

Read the handoff, not the inbox.

You're no longer reviewing everything that arrived. You're reviewing what changed, what it did, and what's still yours.

☀️ Silence never means "fine"

It writes a handoff every weekday morning, even a quiet one: "Nothing needs you today." No handoff on a weekday means something broke — check the task's last run.

📒 Skim the log weekly

Once a week, read what it handled on its own. It's the fastest way to spot a habit you don't like before it becomes a problem.

Why you'd move up

What it can do that the app can't.

⚡
Webhooks

A webhook is a web address another service calls the moment something happens — a form sent, a payment made. With a tool like Zapier making that call, your agent starts right then, not at the next check.

🔌
Any app with an API

Not just the connector directory: any service with an API or an MCP server. Its key is stored so the agent can use it but never see it.

👥
A team of agents
